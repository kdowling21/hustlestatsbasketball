Method

Data

All six seasons come from NBA's own play-by-play feed (7,230 games; 2020-21 was a 72-game season), using a public mirror of that feed. Shot zones use NBA's tags on each shot: restricted area, paint (non-RA), mid-range, corner 3 and above-the-break 3. Backcourt heaves are excluded. Totals match the official box scores, for example 57,768 free throw attempts in 2025-26.

Only trips from shooting fouls count, including and-ones. Bonus fouls, away-from-play hacks, technicals, flagrants and clear-path fouls are excluded. Garbage time is removed for players (teams keep it; see Teams below): 4th-quarter margins of 25+ in the first 3 minutes, 20+ in the next 3, or 15+ in the last 6.

Season views

Single seasons are scored independently, so they can be compared with each other. Last 2, Last 3 and Last 6 add up every attempt with equal weight. That's the best descriptive view, and it roughly halves the noise. Recent-weighted counts attempts at 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.0 by season (the familiar 3-4-5 weighting). It's the best guess at who a player is now. In multi-season views, Pts added is a per-season average.

Baselines

League: everyone in the same seasons and games. Role peers: each player's 20 nearest neighbors on usage, assist %, offensive and defensive rebound %, block %, share of made shots assisted, and minutes per game, all computed from play-by-play. Peers are pooled by attempts, so high-volume peers count most, and the detail panel shows the six that carry the most weight. Adding minutes per game hardly changed how well peers predict shot mix and shooting, but it keeps starters grouped with starters. Height and weight were dropped because they aren't available for every season. Shot location is never an input.

Shrinkage

Each zone FG% and FT% is pulled toward the player's role-peer average: (M + k·peer%) / (A + k). k is estimated from the spread of real talent in the current view. This is the statistically grounded version of pulling extreme numbers back toward the middle: small samples get pulled a lot, big samples barely move. Values for the current view:

Teams

Team scores use the same five parts, built from every attempt by every player, not just the players on the leaderboard. Teams include garbage time, so every possession counts, the same way team ratings are usually reported; the team league baseline includes it too. Each attempt counts for the team the shooter played for in that game, so a player traded midseason splits his shots between both teams. Teams are measured against the league only; role peers don't apply. Team zone FG% and FT% are shrunk toward league average with their own k (table above). Team shooting differences in some zones, especially threes, are mostly luck even over thousands of attempts, so those estimates move a lot toward average. A team's noise range is roughly ±1 point per 100 attempts, so most team gaps of 2 or more are real. Team year-to-year correlation, single seasons:

Against top-10 defenses

Defenses are ranked each season by points allowed per 100 possessions. Each split has its own league baseline. The noise test simulates each player as if he were identical against every opponent.

Positions before 2023-24

Listed positions cover players active from 2023-24 on. For about 180 players whose careers ended earlier, position is estimated from their 10 most similar players on rebounding, blocks, assists, usage, screen assists, speed and shot locations. The estimate matches the listed position 68% of the time and is within one position 98% of the time.

Still not covered

  • Shot difficulty within a zone: defender distance, pull-up vs catch-and-shoot, shot clock.
  • Defenses decide which shots are available, so read Alignment as a signal, not a verdict.
  • Age, team changes, injuries and role changes all drive trends. Check context before calling a trend development.
