What this can't seeMatchup data is useful but imperfect. Read this before leaning on any single number.
  • Help defense lands on the helper. NBA.com credits a shot to whoever is guarding the shooter when he shoots. If a guard gets beat and the center rotates over, the shot counts against the center. That's why raw points allowed isn't used here, and why the lineup-adjusted part (D-RAPM) carries a third of the index.
  • No contest quality. These totals don't record how close the defender was or how good the shot was. A tight contest on a tough make counts the same as an open one. Contest quality is a separate research project.
  • Switches and schemes blur assignments. Matchups are split by time on the floor, so switching teams spread credit around and drop coverages put bigs on a lot of drives. Role and scheme still shape these numbers after comparing within position.
  • Assignment difficulty isn't defense by itself. Guarding stars is a coach's vote of confidence, not proof of stopping them. It's one third of the index, alongside results and D-RAPM.
  • Noise. Single seasons are noisy. D-RAPM starts each season from the player's prior estimate and last season's D-HPM, which steadies it (it repeats year to year at about r = 0.8), but that also means a breakout or a decline shows up gradually. Last 3 seasons is the most reliable view; early in the live season, treat everything as a first look.
  • Coverage. NBA.com's matchup tracking covers about 97% of shots. Off-ball work that never leads to a shot or turnover (denials, deterrence, communication) isn't counted.
